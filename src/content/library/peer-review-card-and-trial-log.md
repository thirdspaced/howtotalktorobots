Two tools for the step between a draft and a classroom. A colleague reads the draft against the source, and one lesson tells you whether to keep it, revise it or stop.

## The peer review card, five minutes

The reviewer reads the source first, then the draft, then answers these in order. Every pair makes at least one revision or says out loud why the draft stands.

1. **Source.** Can I find support for the important claims and the answer key?
2. **Goal.** Does the learner still practice the intended skill?
3. **Access.** Does the support address the stated barrier without stripping out the key idea?
4. **Thinking.** What choice, explanation, comparison or creation is still the learner's?
5. **Usability.** Can the teacher run this in the stated time and format? What will I change?

If the source turns out to be the problem, that's a finding. Write it on the trial log as the first change.

## The one-lesson trial log

- Lesson and date:
- Preparation plus checking time:
- What learners did or said:
- Evidence of understanding:
- Support still needed:
- Keep, revise or stop:
- Next change:

Separate what you saw. Engagement, completion, understanding and independence are four different observations, and none of them is a measured learning gain. One lesson tests the resource. It doesn't prove it works.

## The exit slip

> I changed ______ because ______. In class I will look for ______.

This is the exit slip from the Khan session, one change, one reason, and one thing to look for in the next lesson. It's also how each build in [What survived the classroom](/edubuilds/) is written up.
