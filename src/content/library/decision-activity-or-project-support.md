Two of the three builds in [What survived the classroom](/edubuilds/) started with a version of this prompt: the Oregon Trail-style Gold Rush game for a San Francisco 1849 unit, and the organizers for a place-based water project. Both began as one page.

## Spec first, then the interactive version

Ask for a one-page spec before anything interactive: the setting from the source, three choices, the consequence of each tied to a source fact, one invented rule labeled as invented, and one question that needs evidence. Review the spec. Then ask for the game or the site, if you still want one. Most of the Gold Rush checking happened at this step, and it was most of the build time. If you want to go on to a full game, the [Gold Rush prompt playbook](/library/build-your-own-historical-game/) has the seven prompts that built one.

## The decision activity template

For a smaller version of a big build, one page with these fields: the learning goal, the source facts, the learner choice, the consequence, the explanation question, and one likely misconception. Fill the facts from the source. Label the rest as yours.

## What to check before you use it

- Which information comes from the source and which rule was invented for play. Keep them visibly separate.
- What a learner might take as history or science by mistake. Fix the framing as well as the fact.
- Whose perspectives are in the cast, and who is missing.
- Whether the decisions you listed stayed with learners. If the organizer answers its own questions, send it back.
