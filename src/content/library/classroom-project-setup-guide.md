A project space is where you tell the model about your class once, so you stop re-explaining it in every chat: which sound-spelling patterns you've taught, how long an activity can run, what your readers can handle. This page is the short setup. When you have twenty minutes, the [teaching thought partner setup](/library/teaching-thought-partner-setup/) is the full one, with file checks, a style pass and a teacher check at the end of every resource.

## What a project space is

Most tools have a place where instructions and sources persist across conversations. Claude and ChatGPT call it a Project, Gemini calls it a Gem, and NotebookLM and Copilot call it a notebook. You set it up once per class or unit, and every new chat inside it starts already knowing your context and your rules.

Check which tool your school has approved first. The guide works in any of them.

## Set it up once

1. Create the space and name it after the class or unit, like "AL2 reading, water unit."
2. Paste the instruction block above into the space's instructions, with the brackets filled in from your context card.
3. Attach your sources: the unit plan, public or teacher-written texts, the phonics sequence you teach from, and one or two finished examples you were happy with.
4. Start every build from the context card below, even when the space already has most of it. Saying the goal and the barrier each time keeps the draft tied to that lesson.

## The context card

Fill this in before you ask for anything. It's the top half of the [classroom build card](/library/classroom-build-card/).

- My lesson and age band
- What learners need to explain or do
- The barrier I observed, described as I saw it (not a diagnosis)
- The source or existing resource the draft must come from
- What AI may help create
- What learners must do themselves
- Evidence I will collect

## What goes in

Public or teacher-written sources. The unit plan. The taught phonics sequence. Format and time limits. Finished examples you're happy with.

## What never goes in

Learner names or initials. Diagnoses, IEP or 504 documents. Family communications. Grades. Photos of student work with names visible. If you need the model to account for a learner, describe the barrier you see ("needs one direction at a time," "reads around level L") and the supports you want. The model doesn't need the IEP. It needs the barrier and the goal.

## Keep the last line

Every prompt in this collection ends by asking the model what you should check before using the draft with children. Keep that line when you adapt them. The checking is what makes a draft usable.
